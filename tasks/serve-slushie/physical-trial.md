# Physical trial protocol

Use the same 110 mm tall, 93 mm rim PET cup, starting layout, instruction, 120 s limit and scoring thresholds as the simulation. The procedures below specify a physical replication. The submitted scores and recordings are from Isaac Lab. The hardware options have not been tested on a robot. Qualify the chosen dispenser by hand before collecting trials.

## Slush batch and serving condition

1. Weigh 210 g plain white sucrose and 1,290 g water. Stir until clear. This makes 1,500 g of 14% sucrose solution by mass. For more trials, multiply both masses by the same factor. Keep a covered reserve of this mix. Chill the liquid to 2–4 °C before freezing. Use the same recipe without added ice, juice or syrup. The clear cup can be marked at the target volumes for side-view scoring.
2. For the gravity fixture below, divide the mix between covered, shallow stainless pans to a depth of 15–20 mm. Place in a freezer at about −18 °C. Scrape the bottom and sides into the liquid and stir every 10 min once edge ice forms. Break up lumps until a sample passes a 3 mm perforated spoon with gentle stirring. For the countertop machine, use its slush program and follow its marked minimum and maximum fill levels. Prepare a larger batch at the same concentration if needed.
3. Measure product temperature in a freshly drawn, stirred sample with a probe readable to 0.1 °C. Start at **−1.7 to −1.2 °C** with mobile fine crystals throughout and no solid plug or separate layer of clear syrup. Use the same temperature band for every calibration and trial. If too warm, continue chilling. If too cold or lumpy, temper covered and stir. Recheck after adjusting. Time in the freezer alone does not establish readiness. This band is a protocol target. Commercial granita guidance gives a similar mix concentration and serving temperature. [Icetro service manual, p. 6](https://shop.middleby.com/media/akeneo_connector/asset_files/I/c/Icetro_Slush_Service_Manual__Operation_and_Troubleshooting__V.3_20250818_555c.pdf)
4. Measure **bulk slush density** at serving temperature. Tare a chilled 100 mL straight-sided measure, spoon in a representative sample to the mark without packing, strike level, then weigh immediately. Repeat three times. Divide mean net mass by 100 mL to get `rho` in g/mL. If the three masses span more than 3% of their mean, remix and repeat. Record the measured density for this batch. Keep test material out of food service.

**Set the geometry.** Use the tabletop as the height datum. In the station x/y frame from `task.json`, put the outlet at (385, 0) and **247.5 mm above the table**. Centre the cup beneath it on the grate, **112.5 mm above the table**. This leaves **135 mm from grate to nozzle and 25 mm above the empty cup rim**. Put the pickup platform at (300, 200), 140 mm above the table. Set the lever pivot at (370, 0), 302.5 mm above the table, with its axis along y. At rest the grip points up and 15° toward the robot. Pulling it toward the robot reaches 70° from vertical. Set radius within 2 mm, end angles within 2°, and outlet/stand coordinates and cup clearance within 2 mm. Verify clearance with the actual cup and final gripper covers. Keep brackets and hoses out of the cup and arm paths.

For the gravity feed, prime the outlet, then mark the hopper level corresponding to **900 mL available including the primed wet path**, using the weighed batch and `rho`. Set the shelf so the initial free surface is about 200 mm above the outlet. A 160 mm wide cylindrical hopper drops about 17 mm in head during a 350 mL serving. Lock the shelf after flow calibration. Set about 2.6 mm of cable take-up before the valve begins to open (the first 5° at a 30 mm radius). Across the remaining 50°, a 30 mm sector pulls 26.2 mm, about 83° at an 18 mm valve radius. Use the cable anchor radius, linkage and stops to match the **measured** curve. Guard the linkage. Test that the gripper can hold the lever and that it closes from every test angle without assistance.

**Qualify with slush.** Use a tared catch cup, the batch density and video timing. After several conditioning pulls, collect a steady 2.0 s sample at each angle. Move the catch cup under an already flowing stream for the timed interval. Count residual drips separately in the close test. Repeat three times per angle with the hopper reset and record each result. `flow = net catch mass / (rho × collection time)`.

Repeat the mid- and full-open tests near the **550 mL** remaining inventory typical after a serving. For a commercial unit, always stay within its marked operating range. Use the same marked safe starting inventory each time and report any inventory difference from the 900 mL simulation. Release from full open three times. Require unassisted return to within 5° of the closed mark within 1 s. Log drips for 5 s after closure and include them when weighing actual trials. If full flow is low, inspect for ice bridging and increase outlet bore or head. If high, reduce head or bore. For intermediate errors, adjust the linkage or use a slotted cam with cable take-up set from measured valve travel at the five test angles. Repeat all checks at serving temperature after any change. A fixture that misses a band is not qualified for a comparable physical score.

## Weigh the serving and every spill

Use a 0.1 g resolution bench scale for the cup and collectors. Independently measure **net mass leaving the source** with a platform scale of suitable capacity and 1 g resolution or better. On the gravity fixture weigh the entire primed wet path, including hopper, valve and outlet, before and after. On a commercial machine weigh the appliance with the catch tray supported separately. Keep cable slack and supports identical and take stable, hands-clear readings with no robot or tray force on the source scale. Collect condensation separately. Check scale repeatability with an unused, closed-tap blank for the duration of a trial. If the source reading drifts by more than 2 g, correct the weighing setup before running.

1. Label and tare the empty dry serving cup, removable drip tray **and grate**, secondary catch pan, platform liner, gripper covers and dry recovery wipes in individually marked bags. Include the region beneath the tray and along the carry path. Record source mass after priming and before the first robot action. Run until success or the 120 s limit. Record a side view of the lever and marked cup, and a view of pickup, release and any falls. Keep collecting through 5 s after the scored endpoint to capture delayed drips. Record late drips separately as well as in the physical spill total.
2. At the endpoint photograph the upright cup against volume marks calibrated with measured water volumes in that cup type. Mark **60% and 73%**, about **287 and 350 mL** for 479 mL capacity. If the actual cup differs, measure brim capacity and mark it accordingly. Transfer slush on the **outside** of the cup to a preweighed wipe before weighing the serving. Weigh the cup with its contents. Weigh the tray with grate and retained drips, the secondary pan and each wet cover/liner/wipe. Wipe any remaining splash on the stand, pads or machine exterior into its assigned bag. Account for each deposit once. Weigh the source again at the same cutoff and in the same configuration. Remove and account for exterior splash on the source before its final reading.
3. Let `D = source_before − source_after`, `C = full_cup − dry_cup` and `S = sum(wet − tare)` for **all** spill collectors, including the drip tray. Record `D/rho`, `C/rho` and `S/rho` as dispensed, served and recovered spill in mL of bulk-slush equivalent. Direct cup marks provide an independent fill check. For `D > 0`, the total escaped fraction is `(D − C)/D`, including unrecovered material. With no discharge, record zero volumes and zero spill fraction. Also report `S/D` when defined and the closure error `D − C − S`. If the absolute closure error exceeds the greater of 2 g or 1% of `D`, flag the measurement and repeat after locating the loss. Retain the flagged record and any spill visible on video. A well-measured spill of 5% or more is a failed spill check and stands as scored. If scale uncertainty straddles 5% or the two fill measurements disagree at a scoring boundary, repeat as indeterminate. Only measured spill **strictly below 5%** earns the spill check.

For example, at `rho = 1.05 g/mL`, source loss 350 g, cup gain 336 g, tray/grate gain 10 g and other collectors gaining 4 g give 333.3 mL dispensed, 320.0 mL served and 13.3 mL spilled. Closure error is 0 g and spill is 4.0%. This is a worked example, not a physical trial result.

## Protect the station and reset

Fit thin, close-fitting, washable silicone fingertip sleeves or disposable fitted pad covers. Secure them behind the pad contact face, clear of moving joints and cameras. Use the same covers for calibration and all trials. With the arms in slow supervised mode, check a dry empty cup, then a sealed cup ballasted to the measured serving mass, and check the lever's return. Keep the working tray and a waterproof secondary pan over the entire transport path. Shield exposed electronics outside the arm envelope, preserve vents and keep power connections outside the splash area. If liquid reaches a joint, connector or exposed electronics, stop motion, isolate power per the equipment procedure and have the equipment checked before reuse.

After weighing, remove wet collectors and covers with motion stopped. Clean sticky residue from removable parts with mild detergent, rinse and dry them away from the robot. Wipe fixed surfaces with an equipment-approved damp cloth and dry. Install clean dry covers, tare new collectors, return a clean dry cup to the start mark and verify closed lever and empty tray. Restore the marked starting inventory with the same recipe, recondition and check sample temperature and density. Verify a closed-tap drip test and one full-open flow check, then restore the primed starting inventory and retare before the next trial. Record batch, temperature, density, start inventory, flow, disposition of drips and any resets with the trial video. Physical trial scores are reported separately from the simulated scores.
