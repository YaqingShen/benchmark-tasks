# Reconnect a linear-pull front brake

A small front wheel is already back in its fork on a stationary service stand. Squeeze the two brake arms, seat the curved cable guide in its slotted holder and release the arms. Pull and release the lever to show that the wheel turns, stops and turns again.

The stock moves are illustrated in the [Shimano V-BRAKE user's manual](https://si.shimano.com/en/pdfs/um/8W20A/UM-8W20A-002-ENG.pdf). The scene models a bench test with a nominal 12-inch wheel. Keep the red rim mark in view for the function check. A constant, light drive torque lets the same check be repeated before, during and after braking. The lever pulls a tension-only wire through a compliant housing. The wire bears on the guide, and the brake pads contact the spinning rim. This benchmark does not establish roadworthiness.

The score is based on seating, hands-clear retention and the wheel response to the lever. The robot starts with a disconnected guide. During the function test, hand contact with the wheel invalidates the sequence. A run is evidence of a completed repair only if the score reaches 1.0.

Run the scripted policy with `bash run.sh demo01 850 video` on an activated Isaac Lab-Arena/YAM workstation. For Dream-1, replace `video` with `dream` and activate the Dream inference endpoint. Each run stores the exact source, wheel and lever measurements, and the Inspect result beneath `runs/<name>/`. Debug builds can isolate the arm squeeze or begin with a deliberately seated guide. Those builds always score zero.

**Functional checks.** Under 0.016 N·m of axle torque, the wheel must turn through at least 1 rad above 1 rad/s, stop below 0.15 rad/s for five 20 Hz samples under a right-hand lever pull with both rim pads engaged, and turn through 1 rad again after release. The cable shoulder must stay in its holder after both arms withdraw. The red spoke mark gives the same rotation check from video.

**Scoring.** A seated guide earns 0.25 after the robot compresses both arms and holds the guide against the cradle with its other hand. The guide must remain in contact while spring tension draws the ferrule into its seat. Hands-clear retention earns 0.50. Stopping the previously free wheel using the lever earns 0.75. Free rotation after letting go earns 1.00. Loss of seating clears the retention and braking credit.

The exposed wire is 1.5 mm in diameter. Its top entry slot is 2.0 mm wide. The 4.2 mm guide neck seats axially in a 6.4 mm opening, and the 11 mm shoulder bears on its outside face. The simulation checks those mating surfaces and the guide direction to identify a visibly seated fitting. It also requires finger contact on the guide and both arms during reconnection. The bench uses a 288 mm outside-diameter wheel with a 20 mm-wide brake track. Its 101 mm brake arms pivot on a fixed fork. The rule does not measure stopping distance, rim heating or riding safety.

**Reset.** Mount the wheel in the fork. Leave the lever released, the brake arms open and the cable guide outside the holder. Position the handlebar fixture to the right of the wheel and keep the wheel free of the table. Start both grippers open. The guide shoulder and rim stripe should be visible. A light constant drive replaces an operator turning the wheel by hand. The steel wire is a tension-only model, and the upstream cable housing is modeled as a compliant mount.

**Recorded trials.** Both policies used seed 0 with the same fixed starting layout. Two independent scripted runs scored 1.0 in 738 actions each. The recorded run took 36.90 s of simulation time (about 3 min 36 s elapsed) with an 850-action limit. After both hands cleared the brake assembly the wheel turned, stopped under the lever pull, and turned again after release. In a separate check with the cable guide disconnected, pulling the lever failed to stop the wheel. Dream-1 scored 0.0 at the 850-action limit (42.5 s simulated, about 4 min 7 s elapsed). The task board hosts the setup image and both three-camera recordings.
