# Cold setup and replica calibration

## Make the replica

Use a single clear, kiln-dried American basswood board (Tilia americana) at least 140 × 140 × 22 mm. A 2 × 6 × 6 inch kiln-dried BalsaTron carving blank is large enough. Cut the round from the 6 × 6 inch face, with the grain running along that face. Select stock close to 0.43 g/cm³ by weighing a rectangular blank and measuring its dimensions. A 130 mm × 21 mm cylinder has a volume of 278.7 cm³ and needs a density of 0.431 g/cm³ to weigh 120 g. AHEC reports a species average of 0.417 g/cm³ at 12% moisture, so weigh the finished piece. [Commercial blank](https://www.balsatron.com/products/2-x-6-x-6-basswood-carving-block) · [AHEC species data](https://www.americanhardwood.org/sites/default/files/publications/download/2021-05/8168_AHEC_Species_Guide_March21_update_DIGITAL_Spreads_AW_01_compressed.pdf)

1. Cut a 130 ± 1 mm circle. Plane both faces parallel to 21.0 ± 0.5 mm. Ease the rim by about 0.5 mm. Use straight grain and discard warped or split specimens.
2. Sand both flat faces on the same flat block, ending at P320, with the last strokes along the grain. The sliding surfaces are dry bare wood on both sides. Leave them free of paint, varnish, oil and labels. Mark the circumference near one face with a shallow burned line. Add two small recessed dots on that face near the rim so its orientation is visible from overhead. Keep the marks below the sliding plane.
3. Wash by hand with mild dish soap, rinse, towel dry and air dry on edge. Accept the disk at **120 ± 3 g** when two weighings two hours apart differ by no more than 0.5 g. Repeat after washing and before reuse. Store flat once dry. Record the supplier, measured dimensions, dry mass and room conditions.
4. Use a cold smooth, uncoated aluminum pan interior and a smooth, rigid food-service polypropylene dinner plate. Use the actual utensils intended for the trial. The food-facing side of each utensil contacts the replica. Grip the pan only on the dry silicone-covered handle. Use clean, dry surfaces without fat, detergent residue, non-stick inserts or anti-slip liners. This is a reusable kitchen-handling test object.

## Match the slide before the trial

Mark the grain direction on the rim. Set the grain across the downhill direction for all tests and for the slide back into the skillet. Zero a digital angle gauge on the actual flat food-contact area and put a ruler beside the downhill path. Start with the disk centered. Test the plain and browned faces against the pan and plate, giving four combinations.

For each combination, make three releases from rest. Set a fixed inclination with the disk held by the retractable stop. Release from rest and observe for one second. Reset the disk and increase inclination in 1° steps around onset. Record the lowest fixed angle at which the centre moves at least 2 mm downhill within that second. Then hold the utensil at a fixed 35° and release the centred disk without pushing it, using a thin retractable stop at the downhill edge. Film at 120 fps or faster. Record the release frame and the first frame when the centre has moved 15 mm along the dish. The disk has room to travel 15 mm before hitting the skillet rim. The two tests distinguish initial slip from the subsequent slide.

The 5090 coupon results bracketed slip onset between 21° and 22° on polypropylene and between 22° and 23° on aluminum. The replica moved 15 mm at 35° in 0.110 s on the plate and 0.113 s on the pan. Both faces gave the same rounded results. The flat simulation coupons use the same body, mass and face materials as the task. Each physical test uses the actual plate or skillet to capture its surface finish. The physical comparison bands are 20–24° and 0.090–0.130 s on the plate, and 21–25° and 0.095–0.135 s on the pan. These are proposed build tolerances, allowing about two degrees and two frames at 120 fps. Require a within-face onset spread of at most 3° across the three repeats. These are simulator reference values and tolerance choices. The physical records remain blank until a specimen is tested.

Copy the blank trial JSON into the recording folder, enter observations and filenames, and run `python bench_trial.py trial.json`. The program reports the median and range of the repeats on each face and utensil, then checks the dimensions, mass, slip angle and travel time. Set aside an out-of-range combination and check the washing, dryness, surface finish and actual utensils before using it for comparisons. Preserve the failed measurement with its specimen ID. The calibration tool can run on the Mac and 5090 using Python's standard library.

## Real gripper reference and remaining physical check

The benchmark uses a YAM parallel linear gripper. I2RT describes a 50 N stall force-limiting setting in its driver and lists 95 mm gripper travel and 2 kg nominal arm payload. The official SDK sets the limiter to 50 N when constructing the gripper-equipped arm. The published linear_4310 reference model identifies 50 N per pad. The setting controls motor effort. A contact normal at one pad and the sum of the loads on two opposing pads are different measurements. We report each finger separately and compare each to the reviewer's 40 N value. The simulated skillet, disk and plate weigh 235, 120 and 160 g, respectively. A payload rating alone does not establish grip against the pan torque. [I2RT hardware driver](https://github.com/i2rt-robotics/i2rt/blob/main/i2rt/robots/get_robot.py) · [Linear_4310 reference model](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco_menagerie/blob/main/i2rt_yam/README.md) · [I2RT hardware specifications](https://i2rt.com/products/yam-6-dof-arm)

A loaded skillet has a nominal weight of 3.48 N (0.355 kg × 9.81 m/s²). For a 120 mm horizontal offset between grip and load centre, this gives a static moment of about 0.42 N·m. The actual centre of mass, grip surfaces and acceleration change the required contact forces during a turn. The reference torque is useful for checking a bench setup.

For a future physical confirmation, use the same cookware, a cold hob and a low catch tray, with room to turn the covered pan. Calibrate the grip on the actual handle with a compression gauge in the same jaw gap. Mark the finger positions on the silicone. Film the full turn with a ruler in the handle plane and log the two pad loads if calibrated sensors are available. Run three turnovers and record maximum slip along the handle, achieved inversion angle, release and the final support. Compare to a 3 mm drift allowance and a turn past 150°. Record the gripper variant, gauge uncertainty, force convention and bandwidth. Actual rig measurements are pending.

Grain orientation matters. Published tests on wood and untreated aluminum reported different friction values when sliding along and across the face grain. Their aluminum finish, wood and contact loads differ from this kitchen setup. Use the actual dish and specimen for qualification. [Friction measurements, Table 4.2](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000176322/155599149)

For a future instrumented test, copy `grip-trial.template.json`, supply the three videos and the original time series with columns `time_s,left_pad_N,right_pad_N`, and run `python grip_trial.py trial.json`. Capture the complete handling interval, including grip and release, at 240 Hz or faster. Enter the stated measurement uncertainty. The tool checks measured peak plus uncertainty for each individual pad, the filmed angle, slip and released end state.
