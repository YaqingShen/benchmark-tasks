# Physical setup and commissioning for the milk trial

## Cold chain and reset

Use one unopened, in-date 64 US fl oz (1.89 L) refrigerated LACTAID Whole Milk carton with the molded pull ring per attempt. Carry cartons upright in an insulated cooler with cold packs. Log the lot, use-by date and receipt temperature. Reject damaged, swollen, leaking or previously opened cartons and cartons with an unknown cold chain. Store upright at 1–4 °C in a refrigerator with a min/max logger. Check the log before testing. Set up the dry station first.

Run in a room at 20–22 °C. The milk must be 1–4 °C at both ends of the trial. Use a sanitized food probe with accuracy of ±0.3 °C or better. Check it in a stirred ice-water bath before the session. For the start reading, fully equilibrate an identical same-lot companion carton beside the trial carton on the same shelf (at least 12 hours). Remove them together, gently mix and measure the companion at mid-depth; record this explicitly as a start-temperature proxy. Retain the trial carton sealed until setup. Use a fresh companion for each attempt. After the robot stops, transport the upright test carton in its pan to the cleanup area and immediately measure the remaining milk in the trial carton itself. If the seal is still intact, the operator opens it there. If a spill precludes a representative end sample, mark the temperature unverified and the physical run invalid. Record a value within 1.3–3.7 °C when using a ±0.3 °C probe, so the instrument tolerance fits the specified range.

Start a timer at refrigerator removal. Begin robot motion within three minutes. The 1,800-step limit at 20 Hz is 90 seconds. Finish the post-trial reading within ten minutes of removal, including setup, pauses and transport. Record start/end times, air temperature, both milk readings, probe ID, operator and any excursion. Stop and discard if the limit is exceeded or a reading or cold-chain record is out of range. Keep spare cartons refrigerated. The reset operator washes hands, uses fresh clean gloves, dries condensation on the exterior and removes only the outer cap by its outside. Place the cap inner face up on its clean rest. Discard both cartons after the attempt. Never consume or return trial milk to stock. Segregate dairy-contact equipment and dispose of milk and used liners under the facility's food-waste procedure.

These are tighter experiment-specific limits than the general FDA limits. FDA recommends refrigerated storage at or below 40 °F and limiting unrefrigerated time for perishables. The manufacturer specifies refrigeration for this milk. ## Protect both grippers and dry equipment

Before bringing liquid to the station, power down the arms for installation. Fit both parallel grippers with intact, washable, liquid-impermeable silicone or TPU boots with separate snug, closed fingertip pockets. Enclose each pad, finger slide and the gripper housing. Overlap the wrist splash sleeve over the boot cuff in the runoff direction and secure on a stationary, approved cuff land. Keep seams off the contact pads and loose material away from pinch points. Route camera leads and connectors upward behind a splash cover with drip loops; keep camera windows clear. Keep vents clear and follow the robot vendor's cover, temperature and joint-travel limits. If the end effector cannot be covered without obstructing a vent, camera or joint, do not run milk on that rig.

Match the fitted outer pad profile to the modeled gripper using a spare pad or fitted insert. Check jaw travel, ring entry and hook transfer on a dry sacrificial empty carton. At normal force limits, verify no slip with the tool and a representative pull load using a fixture and a spring scale through 24 N. Use an instrumented dummy load away from personnel; record each jaw pair, peak and any slip. With powered hardware absent, cycle the fitted covers on a dummy gripper through full jaw travel, wet the exterior and inspect a dry witness inside fingers, slides and cuff. Replace torn, loose, wet-inside or incompatible covers. Save the check results before commissioning the real rig.

## Full-carton spill containment

Install a single-piece or continuously welded, washable, nonporous pan with a 600 × 800 mm **clear internal** footprint. Its lowest continuous rim is 40 mm above its bottom. No drains or fasteners penetrate the wet area. The far wall and both ends rise to 160 mm to intercept splash. Fix the pan against sliding and level to within 2 mm across either axis. In the simulation the inner floor coincides with the station worktop datum; on the bench set the pan floor at the calibrated working height and re-register the robots to it. Robot bases and electrical connections sit outside the wet zone. Use separate removable splash shields around exposed base connectors and protect any adjacent camera electronics without blocking vision, travel, vents or emergency access.

With the origins used in the model, the inner bounds are x = 95–695 mm and y = −400 to +400 mm. Place the carton, both rests, hook and discard tray entirely inside, including their transport paths. The discard tray receives only the removed seal and contributes no credited spill capacity. Keep all absorbent witnesses, fixtures and the fallen carton within a combined footprint of at most 0.120 m²; do not add a bulky liner that reduces capacity. At 20 mm liquid depth the conservative usable volume is (0.600 × 0.800 − 0.120) × 0.020 = 0.00720 m³, or 7.20 L. The remaining 20 mm to the lowest rim is reserved for tilt and splash. Seal a clear splash enclosure to the whole perimeter of the pan. The 550 mm height is measured from its floor to the underside of its closed lid. Use 5 mm clear polycarbonate side panels, a gasketed lid secured by external clamps, and continuous liquid-tight gaskets on every joint. On the arm side keep the 40 mm metal rim continuous. Above it fit two separately sealed pass-throughs, one per arm, on a rigid perimeter frame with a central upright. Clamp each flexible, impervious rolling sleeve around its port flange with continuous gaskets and clamp rings. Terminate its moving end on the fitted liquid-tight gripper boot, leaving the jaws independently movable. In the wet zone the robot remains inside the dry lumen of this continuous barrier from port to covered fingers. Use a sealed optical window for each wrist camera. The dry-side bases, cables and connectors stay outside. No open arm slot is allowed. The lid opens only for setup, with motion inhibited. Dry-cycle the full trajectory to check cuff travel, sight lines, sleeve slack, trapped folds and emergency stops. Inspect all ports, corners and the lid after the water challenge. Do not use the unhooded pan on the shared rig.

Nest the assembly in an independent, one-piece stainless secondary pan without drains. The secondary pan has a 680 × 880 mm clear interior, a 30 mm lip and a continuous 40 mm apron outside the inner pan on all four sides. Its floor is 3 mm below the inner floor. Do not pierce either pan to mount the hood or the robot. Put the outside witnesses beyond the secondary rim and underneath its base. Put separate witnesses on the inter-pan apron so that an inner seam leak can be identified. Check both pans individually with their actual fixtures. For the secondary proof, place the empty inner box in the outer pan and fill the accessible annulus with measured water. At 20 mm fill, the annular volume alone is (0.680 × 0.880 − 0.600 × 0.800) × 0.020 = 2.368 L before material displacement. Require an actual measured capacity of at least 2.10 L at or below 20 mm; record it. Dry both pans before the dynamic checks. Investigate and reseal any inner seam that wets the apron during physical commissioning.

Before the first milk trial and after any change to the setup, close and latch the hood and perform an unpowered water challenge away from live shared equipment. Use the installed pan and props, dummy grippers and dummy bases in the closest working poses. Put dry weighed witnesses below the pan, outside each rim, inside the boots, at each port flange, lid joint and at the dummy base, camera and connector locations. Verify liquid-tight seams with at least 2.50 L measured water in the occupied pan for five minutes. Then empty and dry it. Tip a surrogate full carton from its trial position toward each of the four edges in separate 1.89 L releases, including the low arm-side rim, at the trial mouth height. Open the surrogate sufficiently to dump the complete charge. Use a fresh dry witness set and recover the water from the pan and wet-side props after each challenge. Require no visible droplets outside or behind protection, no external witness gain above balance repeatability (use a 0.1 g balance), no dampness inside the boot, and recovery within 1% after accounting for water left in the surrogate. Record charge mass, tare and final masses, direction, largest fill depth, photographs and any loss. If any check fails, modify the containment and repeat all challenges. An uncommissioned rig is not ready for a milk trial.

In a trial, any escaped milk counts toward the existing 1 mL spill threshold even if the pan catches it. For a spill stop robot motion, keep the carton over the pan and follow the site isolation procedure from the dry side before approaching the rig. If liquid reaches a boot interior, base, camera connector or electrics, remove the equipment from service for inspection. Remove pan contents with a ladle or transfer pump before lifting the pan. Remove covers without transferring milk to the pads. Wash, rinse, sanitize and dry the pan, hook, tray and covers with materials-compatible food-contact products. Clean shared surfaces using the manufacturer's procedure. Inspect for residue, damage or trapped moisture before restoring power.
