# Wet enclosure build and dry fit

Use a removable 304 stainless pan, continuously welded at the corners or drawn in one piece. Measure the internal dimensions after fabrication. The pan interior is 600 × 800 mm, with a continuous 40 mm minimum lip. Extend the two ends and far side to 160 mm. Deburr and round exposed edges. Carry the enclosure and pan on supports rated for their measured installed weight plus a full liquid charge. Fix them against tipping or sliding. The separate inner discard tray has no credited containment volume.

Above the pan, install a gasketed 5 mm clear polycarbonate hood. From the pan floor to the underside of the lid is 550 mm. Place the removable lid in a continuous external clamping frame. Overlap the hood with the pan rim so runoff returns inside. Use continuous compatible compression gaskets; do not depend on absorbent pads or adhesive tape to seal a through-hole. Keep fasteners above the rim or outside the wet boundary. The two arms enter from the same 800 mm side. Divide the panel above its 40 mm rim into two 400 mm bays with port centers at y = +200 and −200 mm. A 10 mm flange inset on all sides of each bay gives a nominal 380 × 490 mm clear port, centered 295 mm above the pan floor. Close each entire port with a flanged, washable waterproof bellows terminating in the gripper boot. Seal the 20 mm central frame. The dry side of each sleeve opens toward the robot base; its wet-facing end is closed at both fingertips. Use a clear closed optical window wherever a wrist camera looks through the cover.

Use a commercial or custom liquid-tight robotic gauntlet of silicone or welded TPU compatible with the site cleaner. Select length from the actual dry trajectory. Start with at least 650 mm deployed length and require at least 50 mm free extension at the farthest pose. Use compliant gussets and return folds that cannot enter the jaw mechanism. Fit the protected fingertips to the nominal outside pad geometry; check actual tip width, jaw opening and clear space through the 20 mm ring with calipers. Record three successful tool grasps and direct ring transfers on a dry practice fixture before testing with liquid. Record whether either sleeve tugs, slips, contacts the work or blocks a camera. Stop at the first interference. Validate the grip under the prescribed load and check for leaks inside each glove and sleeve after installation and replacement.

The simulated membrane stays impermeable as the corresponding arm crosses its port. The rigid panels remain colliders. The simulation does not resolve individual bellows folds, seal pressure or leakage through material defects. Test these on the assembled structure using dummy limbs and cameras, the water challenges and dry witnesses described in wet-trial.md before energizing shared hardware.

Nest the primary unit in a separate seamless secondary pan (680 × 880 mm measured internally, height 30 mm, 3 mm floor offset). The 40 mm apron dimension in the simulation is measured between the inner faces of the two pans. With 3 mm primary walls, the unobstructed width is 37 mm on each side. Allow for the external footprint, including brackets. With 3 mm primary walls its 606 × 806 mm exterior leaves about 2.20 L in the clear annulus at a 20 mm fill, before brackets or additional equipment. Verify the actual minimum of 2.10 L with the primary assembly installed. The secondary base extends beneath the entire inner unit. Route cables and the dry side of both arm ports clear of the outer lip. During commissioning inspect separate witnesses at the inner seam, in the secondary apron and beyond the outer rim.
